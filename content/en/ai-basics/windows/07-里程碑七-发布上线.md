## What's the problem with this?

It's not really delivered. Turning online would force you to face open web sites, build differences, real equipment and user feedback, and this would turn “I made it” into “others can use it”.

## What will you finish?

- Complete project and version records and complete a further public deployment.
- After closing local projects, the site was validated with another device and different networks.
- Invite a real user to try out, record problems and complete a minor one.

## Criteria for success

You have a publicly accessible web site; mobile phones can be opened independently and complete core games; and at least one real user can start using them without your live explanation.

## Capacity migration

When you publish websites, documents or in-house tools later, you distinguish between "local running, building success, online accessibility, and the ability of users to do their job" and not just the greening of water lines.
